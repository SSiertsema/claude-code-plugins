# Human Skills

Skills that require human involvement and cannot be fully performed by LLM agents.

---

## Phase 1: Vision & Strategy

### Market Research & Analysis
- Survey design & analysis — *Analysis yes; administering surveys to real respondents requires human distribution and collection*

### Stakeholder Identification
- Stakeholder interviews — *Requires live conversation with real stakeholders to elicit tacit knowledge, politics, and unspoken concerns*
- Expectation management — *Requires ongoing human relationship management, negotiation, and political navigation*

---

## Phase 2: Exploration & Validation

### Ideation & Concept Development
- Design thinking workshops — *Requires facilitation of live group dynamics, reading the room, and real-time collaboration between human participants*
- Crazy Eights — *Time-boxed rapid sketching exercise requiring human hand-drawing and spatial thinking under pressure*
- Dot voting — *Requires real participants expressing genuine preferences; agent-simulated votes have no decision-making legitimacy*

### User Research
- Contextual inquiry — *Requires observing real users in their actual environment to understand situated behavior and workarounds*
- Semi-structured interviews — *Requires live conversation with real users to surface unanticipated insights through follow-up and rapport*
- Ethnographic observation — *Requires physical presence in the user's environment to observe real behavior, culture, and context*
- Diary studies — *Requires real participants logging their experiences over time in natural settings*
- Card sorting — *Requires real users organizing content to reveal their mental models; agent-generated sorts reflect training data, not user cognition*
- Survey design (quantitative) — *Design yes; administering to and collecting responses from real populations requires human distribution*
- Focus groups — *Requires moderated group discussion with real participants to capture social dynamics and emergent opinions*

### Feasibility Study
- Technical spike / proof of technology — *Requires running real code against real infrastructure to validate technical assumptions*
- Capacity analysis — *Requires access to actual team availability, organizational capacity data, and HR constraints*
- Skills gap analysis — *Requires knowledge of actual team members' skills and experience levels*

### Regulatory & Compliance Analysis
- Compliance gap analysis — *Framework yes; accurate gap analysis requires access to actual organizational controls, policies, and audit evidence*
- Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA) — *Template yes; valid assessment requires knowledge of actual data processing activities and sign-off by a DPO or legal authority*
- Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) — *Template yes; valid assessment requires knowledge of actual data processing activities and sign-off by a DPO or legal authority*

### Risk Management
- Risk identification workshops — *Requires facilitated group sessions with domain experts who hold tacit knowledge of organizational and technical risks*

### Proof of Concept (PoC)
- Technical spike implementation — *Requires deploying and running real code against real infrastructure to validate technical feasibility*
- Wizard of Oz testing — *Requires a human operator to manually simulate system behavior behind the scenes with real users*
- Concierge MVP — *Requires a human to manually deliver the service to real customers to validate demand*
- Smoke test / landing page test — *Requires deploying a real page and measuring actual user traffic and conversion*

## Phase 4: Experience Design

### Information Architecture
- Card sorting (open & closed) — *Requires real users organizing content to reveal their mental models; agent-generated sorts reflect training data, not user cognition*
- Tree testing — *Requires real users navigating a proposed hierarchy to measure findability; synthetic results lack validity*

### Accessibility Design
- Screen reader testing — *Requires running actual screen reader software against a real UI to verify announcements, focus order, and interaction*
- Cognitive load assessment — *Meaningful assessment requires observing real users with diverse cognitive abilities interacting with the interface*

### Visual / UI Design
- Icon design / selection — *Selection yes; original icon design requires visual craftsmanship and pixel-level output that text-based agents cannot produce*
- Motion / animation design — *Specification yes; crafting and tuning actual motion requires visual tools and real-time preview of timing/easing*
- Brand alignment review — *Checklist review yes; subjective brand perception judgment requires human visual and emotional assessment*

### Prototyping
- Design tool mastery (Figma, Sketch, Adobe XD) — *Requires direct manipulation of visual design tools with real-time pixel-level control and visual judgment*

### Usability Testing
- Moderated usability testing — *Requires a facilitator guiding real users through tasks, probing reactions, and adapting questions in real time*
- Unmoderated remote testing — *Requires real users completing tasks independently; the value is in observing genuine human behavior*
- Think-aloud protocol — *Requires real users verbalizing their thought process while interacting with the product*
- Task success rate measurement — *Requires real users attempting tasks to produce valid success/failure metrics*
- System Usability Scale (SUS) — *Requires real users filling out the standardized questionnaire after using the product*
- A/B testing — *Requires deploying variants to real user traffic and measuring actual behavioral differences*
- First-click testing — *Requires real users clicking on an interface to measure where they naturally navigate first*
- Eye tracking analysis — *Requires specialized hardware tracking real users' eye movements on a physical screen*
